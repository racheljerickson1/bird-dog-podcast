Transcript

Full transcript for Episode 48: What is the Greyhound Protection Act & how it can affect you! Get involved today!.

Tyce

Hey everyone, welcome to the Bird Dog Podcast. My name is Tyce Erickson. Today I want to talk about something that's been circulating online and directly affects everyone who trains or hunts with dogs — the Greyhound Protection Act. I dove into this before recording so the information I'm sharing should be accurate, but I'm not a lawyer, so I'd encourage you to look into it yourself and take action if it concerns you.

The Greyhound Protection Act of 2025, sponsored by a Democratic representative from California, was inserted into a larger piece of legislation called the Farm, Food and National Security Act of 2026. The bill number is HR 5017. The organizations backing it include Grey2K USA Worldwide and Animal Wellness Action — groups focused on ending greyhound racing and, more broadly, what they call animal welfare concerns in dog-related activities.

Greyhound racing itself is essentially already gone — it's been banned in about 40 states, and only West Virginia still has active tracks. On the surface, this bill targets that remaining racing industry. The concern for hunting and dog training comes from the specific language buried in it. The bill makes it unlawful to engage in "live lure training" — and the definition of that phrase is broad enough to potentially cover using live birds in dog training.

If you train bird dogs — upland dogs, retrievers, versatile breeds — you use live birds. Pigeons, pheasants, ducks, chukars. That's been part of the process for hundreds of years. It's not entertainment; it's how you build drive, develop pointing instinct, train retrievers to mark and handle. If this live lure language passes into federal law, it could affect anyone using live birds to train dogs, including professional trainers, hunt test competitors running live-bird tests, and field trial participants.

The tactic being used here is familiar: take a bill that sounds like it's about one thing — greyhound racing, something most people have no stake in — and insert language broad enough to reach into your world without you noticing. If it gets attached to a major farm bill and passes, it becomes federal law, and the enforcement implications could be significant.

What can you do? Contact your representatives. Let them know you're aware of this language and that it matters to you. Support organizations like Pheasants Forever, NSTRA, AKC, and other sporting dog groups that are tracking this legislation and pushing back. Don't assume someone else will handle it. These bills move when the public isn't paying attention.

I don't know exactly where this stands right now, but I think it's worth knowing about and worth making noise over. The hunting and dog training community has a voice — use it. Thanks for listening.
