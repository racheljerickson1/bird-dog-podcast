Transcript

Full transcript for Episode 25: (EP:25) What kind of personality category does your dog fall into?.

Tyce

Hey everyone, welcome to the Bird Dog Podcast. My name is Tyce. After 19-plus years of training dogs, I've noticed that most dogs fall into recognizable personality patterns — and knowing which category your dog lands in can completely change how you approach training. Today I want to walk through what I call the canine personality breakdown: six categories based on two variables, confidence level and responsiveness to pressure.

Let's start with the hardest dog to train: low confidence, not responsive to pressure. This dog is what most people call "soft and stubborn." It shuts down under any kind of stress, acts like the world is ending when you correct it, and then also doesn't work through the correction — it just freezes or goes into avoidance. Soft dogs that are also not responsive to pressure are the longest training projects I encounter. You can't push them hard because they shut down, but you also can't let them avoid work because they're stubborn. It takes enormous patience, very gradual exposure, and a lot of confidence-building before you can move forward. These dogs exist, but they're not common, which is fortunate.

Next: low confidence but responsive to pressure. This is still a soft dog — it may go slinky if you raise your voice, and it needs to be handled carefully — but when you apply training pressure, it works through it. That's the key difference. This dog will come around. You work at a lower stimulation level on the e-collar, do more reps, build confidence gradually, and over time it will develop. Most of these dogs end up becoming solid workers. The softness just means you have to take your time and be thoughtful.

The mid-range category: mid confidence, responsive to pressure. This is your well-balanced dog. Not too hot, not too soft, understands the concept of pressure, works through corrections, maintains a good attitude. This dog trains out well and tends to be easy to live with. If you can pick one, this is what you're looking for. The mid-range dog that is not responsive to pressure — stubbornness at average confidence — is a trickier case. The dog seems normal externally but digs in when you try to enforce anything. More pressure is usually required, and finding the right level takes some work.

Then you have high confidence, responsive to pressure. This is one of the best dogs to train. It has the energy, the drive, the boldness — and when you correct it, it bounces back immediately and keeps working. Bounce back is the key trait I watch for in high-confidence dogs. Correct it and it shakes it off and goes right back to work with a good attitude. That combination of confidence and bounce back is what makes an elite performance dog. These dogs absorb a lot of training because they're resilient. They want to work and they don't hold corrections against you.

The last category is high confidence, not responsive to pressure. High drive, big personality, doesn't respond to correction. This is challenging in a different way from the soft dog — the confident dog without pressure response will just keep doing what it wants to do, and you can run out of tools. Finding what that dog responds to — whether it's a different method, a different collar, a different approach entirely — becomes the puzzle. These dogs can be trained, but they require an experienced handler who won't get frustrated or escalate unnecessarily.

Why does this matter? Because when you understand where your dog is in this breakdown, you can calibrate your training approach. A soft dog needs confidence-building first; too much pressure too fast creates shutdown. A stubborn dog needs more consistent, firm enforcement; backing off rewards the stubbornness. A high-confidence dog needs enough challenge and structure to stay engaged. Picking a puppy with all of this in mind is worth the effort — watch how it responds to mild pressure in the litter, how it bounces back, how it interacts with its littermates. Most dogs don't surprise you if you watch carefully early. Thanks for listening.
